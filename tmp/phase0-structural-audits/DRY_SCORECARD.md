## Scoring Rationale
- `0-39`: severe DRY or ownership failure. Usually a barrel, a catch-all facade, a huge shim layer, or an over-broad package that mixes ownership boundaries.
- `40-59`: meaningful duplication or boundary drift, but still salvageable with a first split or canonical owner extraction.
- `60-79`: moderate overlap or proof/runtime churn. Usually has a real owner, but the shape is too wide, too duplicated, or too proof-heavy.
- `80-100`: narrow contract ownership, low duplication, and clean dependency direction.
- Component scores are higher when the audit evidence points to one canonical owner and lower when the audit points to mirrored shapes, frontage shims, or proof/runtime overreach.

## No-Reexport Coordination Standard
- Re-exports/barrels are banned and must not be replaced with new re-export or compatibility shims.
- Forbidden shapes: Rust `pub use`, `pub(crate) use`, `pub(super) use`; TypeScript `export *`, `export { X } from`; alias re-export files; root frontage barrels; barrel-like hop modules.
- Acceptable cleanup shape: direct imports from the owning source module.
- Rust public access shape: expose the owning module itself with `pub mod owning_module;`, then import `crate::owning_module::Thing` or external `crate_name::owning_module::Thing` directly.
- If a packet cannot avoid a shim or re-export within the path cap, report BLOCKED or provide a split plan rather than adding debt.

## Current Refresh Audit - 2026-06-19
- Responsibility: This crate is the child-policy decision and handoff core. It should own tracking-policy evaluation, child-domain policy evaluation, and the narrow adapters that turn those decisions into policy-control request and delivery records.
- Deps: The dependency set is intentionally small and matches the declared boundary: `ocentra-eventing`, `ocentra-parent-agent-protocol`, and `ocentra-policy-control-core`. The risk is not excess dependencies, it is repeated logic layered on top of those owners.
- Violations: No forbidden re-export or direct dependency violation is visible. The real structural violations are ownership drift and duplicate transformation shape, especially where this crate repeats canonicalization and status mapping that belongs in one place.
- Duplicated shapes: `TrackingNearbyPlacePolicyDecision` and `TrackingExpectedPlacePolicyDecision` are the same shape; both `*_not_detected` helpers are the same shape; `PolicyControlRequestHandoffReport` and `PolicyControlDeliveryHandoffReport` are thin wrapper records; `tracking_policy.rs` and `child_domain_policy.rs` are both one-way event-to-decision transforms.
- Barrel/reexport/shim debt: No `pub use` / `export` shim debt was found. `src/lib.rs` is only a module barrel, but the handoff files are effectively pass-through shims over `ocentra-policy-control-core` and are the place most likely to accrete more duplication.
- Schema drift: `policy_control_request.rs` hardcodes request and submission prefixes and locally maps origin to status / confirmation state. That makes this crate vulnerable to upstream schema drift if `policy-control-core` changes request semantics, prefixes, or origin-state mapping.
- Event/log/proof misuse: There is no logger or proof emission surface here, which is acceptable for a pure policy core. The misuse risk is downstream: these helpers are pure transforms, so they must not be treated as proof or logging boundaries by callers.
- Test/proof structure issues: The tests are useful but narrow. They cover the happy path and a few rejection paths, but they do not lock the prefix format, the origin-to-status mapping, or the adapter boundaries with a dedicated contract/proof harness.
- Score: 6/10. The crate is coherent and small, but it still behaves like a thin duplication layer around policy-control-core rather than a strongly normalized policy core.
- Fix recommendation: Pull repeated normalization into one canonical helper, keep the request/delivery adapters minimal, and move any shared request-state mapping into the owning policy-control crate instead of re-encoding it here.
- Decouple recommendation: Split evaluation-only policy transforms from request/delivery handoff wrappers more aggressively. Keep `tracking_policy.rs` and `child_domain_policy.rs` as pure decision layers, and isolate request/delivery construction into a single adapter module with one shared normalization path.
- Blockers: No runtime blocker. This refresh stayed within the read-only boundary, the target audit file was claimable, and the target crate was readable end to end.
- Exact likely paths: `crates/child-policy-core/src/lib.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/src/tracking_policy.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/src/child_domain_policy.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/src/policy_control_request.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/src/policy_control_request_handoff.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/src/policy_control_delivery_handoff.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/tests/unit.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/tests/unit/tracking_policy.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/tests/unit/child_domain_policy.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/tests/unit/policy_control_request.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/tests/unit/policy_control_request_handoff.rs`; `crates/child-policy-core/tests/unit/policy_control_delivery_handoff.rs`.
